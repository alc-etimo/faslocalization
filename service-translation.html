Case study 01 · Translation + spotting

Timed subtitles from scratch, read against the picture.

No script. No template. The brief, the source video, and a native pt-BR linguist who spots, translates, and QCs in one pass.

EN → PT-BRFilm & documentarySpottingQCSRT / VTT

Spotting makes or breaks a subtitle.

The same line, moved 200ms earlier or later, stops fitting the shot. A cue that starts two frames before a hard cut flashes on screen for a moment that the editor never intended — and the viewer feels it, even if they can't name it.

This is the pass that turns a translation into a subtitle. Timing follows the picture; translation follows the timing.

What the workflow looks like.

1. Read the brief. Glossary, style guide, CPS, CPL, line count. If the brief is silent, ask — never guess a number onto a broadcast job.

2. Spot. A first pass against the picture, not the script. Shot changes and reading speed define the frame.

3. Translate. Into spoken pt-BR, inside the frame the spotting pass gave us. Idioms, jokes, on-screen text adapted.

4. Watch it. The file goes back on the picture. Timing, spelling, shot changes, forced narratives. Nothing leaves unread.
