Case study 02 · Timed translation

Fitting the words into the timing, not the other way around.

A pre-timed SRT lands on your desk. The timing is locked. Your job is to translate it into natural pt-BR while staying inside the CPS and CPL limits of the brief — without touching a single frame.

Translation is a spatial problem.

When the timing is already set, you can't just translate the sentence — you have to translate it into the space the cue provides. If the sentence is too long for the duration, the viewer won't finish reading it before it disappears.

The editor below simulates this constraint. You have a single cue. The brief has strict limits. You can type freely, but the metrics update live to show you when you drift out of bounds. The automatic tools let you fix formatting and timing issues with a single click.

Try the editor.

Type in the PT-BR field to translate the source. Change the brief profile in the toolbar. Use the buttons below the text area to fix line lengths or split the cue if the reading speed is too high.

How the pass works.

01

Read the brief

Know the limits before you type. A streaming brief allows more CPS than a cinema brief. The tool adapts to the profile you select.

02

Translate against the time

Don't translate the sentence, translate the slot. If the Portuguese is too long, find a shorter equivalent rather than cutting words later.

03

Fix with tools, not guesses

If the CPS is high, split the cue. If the CPL is too long, split the line. The tools do exactly one thing, so you can trust the result.
