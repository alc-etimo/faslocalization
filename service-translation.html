Spotting is where translation becomes a subtitle.

The same line, moved 200 ms earlier or later, stops fitting the shot. A cue that starts two frames before a hard cut flashes on screen for a moment the editor never intended — and the viewer feels it, even if they can't name it.

This demo shows the spotting step in a subtitle editor mock. The source audio and video are illustrative; the waveform and the cues are generated in the browser, so you can drag, retime, and see CPS and CPL update live.

How the pass works.

01

Read the picture first

Shots, cuts, on-screen text. The timing has to respect what the frame is doing before a single word is placed.

02

Spot the cue

In and out points land on the breath, not on the syllable. A cue that crosses a cut gets split or nudged.

03

Translate to the timing

The pt-BR line is written to fit the slot — not trimmed afterwards. CPS and CPL are constraints, not cleanup.

04

QC against playback

Watch the cue in context, check the gap to the next one, and export SRT or VTT.
